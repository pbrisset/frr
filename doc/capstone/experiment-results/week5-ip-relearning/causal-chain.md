# Clock-qualified causal reconstruction

## Gateway intervention (`raw-gateway-02`)

PCAP timestamps and `ip -ts monitor` logs use realtime within the shared Linux time namespace; the monitor timestamps are userspace observation times, with no measured clock error. CLI and attachment observations use monotonic. The original analyzer's separate `timeline.svg` and `timeline-bgp.svg` preserve the clock boundary. Sequence and time support a causal explanation; internal process wakeups, network queues and netlink delivery time are not directly traced.

1. Before the move, Leaf2 snapshots show MAC and IP remote via Leaf1; kernel neighbor is Zebra `extern_learn`, `NOARP`. The single veth is detached then moved and attached to Leaf2; see attachment events. Controller probes and incidental ARP continue.
2. Leaf1 capture shows Type-2 MAC/IP withdrawal at realtime 1793764412.134311; Leaf2 capture shows MAC-only announcement 1793764412.159336. On Leaf2, `ip monitor` logs failed then deleted 45.0.0.11 neighbor at 03:53:32.196868–.196908. Leaf5 sees withdrawal and its /32 deletion at 03:53:32.246848. Different observation points, capture direction and TCP handling limit exact internal ordering. Leaf2 samples its MAC local/seq 1, IP absent; the last pre-move sample had external remote IP. It does not prove the externally learned entry survived every ARP or caused Linux not to replace it.
3. Leaf5's /24 ECMP remains and its sequential lookup/RMAC/FDB sample resolves Leaf3. This is network forwarding state, not controller application location knowledge.
4. Gateway intervention starts at worker time recorded in `host11-stimulus.log`; robot and Leaf2 PCAPs show Leaf2's gateway ARP request and robot reply. Leaf2 neighbor `REACHABLE` monitor event at 03:53:35.127560 precedes captured Leaf2 MAC/IP Type-2 announcements at 1793764415.178507 and .178548 to separate link-local peers. Leaf5 captures the announcement and monitor logs /32 via Leaf2 at 03:53:35.290889. First later snapshots verify Leaf2 local neighbor and Leaf5 matching resolution. No claim of exact latency between realtime and monotonic clocks is made.
5. In `raw-none-01`, failed controller probe ICMP ID 42000 seq 60 is captured at controller frame 102, Leaf5 frames 522–526, VXLAN VNI 500 outer destination 10.0.0.3; Leaf3 frames 105–107 show receipt. No matching packet is seen in Leaf2/host11 captures, nor reply at controller. Capture drops are not fully accounted for, so the exact drop location and whether ARP, FDB, or another cause blocked onward delivery are UNKNOWN. Classified as observed wrong first VTEP with failed probe, final drop UNKNOWN.
6. Counterexample `raw-repeated-01` seq 60: Leaf5 frame 621 sends VNI 500 to Leaf3, Leaf3 frame 146 sends VNI 1000 onward to Leaf2, Leaf2 frames 206–213, host11 frames 128–129, and controller frame 122 show request and reply. An unrelated first VTEP can still deliver by L2 forwarding.
7. In `raw-delayed-01` seq 64, Leaf5 frame 580 and Leaf3 frame 107 receive VNI 500 before the delayed GARP; Leaf3 frame 149 sees GARP from Leaf2 and frame 210 then sends the same probe in VNI 1000 to Leaf2; host11 frames 137–138 and controller frame 138 show request/reply. Leaf2 IP and Leaf5 /32 remain absent in later snapshots. Packet buffering pending neighbor resolution is plausible but not directly proven. This is reachability recovery without demonstrated /32 restoration.

A gateway/ARP exchange restoring the route is supported in one intervention, while repeated and delayed self-target GARPs show delivery may recover through an alternative route without Leaf2 IP learning. Neither proves a universal recovery mechanism.
