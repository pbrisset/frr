# Cisco FRR EVPN robot mobility: research handoff through Week 5

## Purpose and architecture

The project investigates warehouse robot movement across an EVPN-VXLAN fabric, including controller reachability and mobility control-plane behavior. Our emulator uses Linux namespaces, bridges, VXLAN and FRRouting: two spines, five leaves, eight robot hosts behind leaves1–4, and a controller host `20.0.0.51` behind leaf5. Robot tenant subnet is `45.0.0.0/24`, L2VNI 1000 and tenant VRF/L3VNI 500. Each leaf peers with both spines using eBGP unnumbered and exchanges EVPN. The typical moving identity is host11, `45.0.0.11`, MAC `00:00:00:00:00:11`, initially leaf1 and moved to leaf2. The controller in these tests is a host sending ICMP; its application-level location database was not measured.

Our BBM harness detaches and transfers one veth; command completion times bound a real disconnection. Phase 1 MBB creates a second same-MAC veth while the old link stays attached, sends raw gratuitous ARP, then transfers the sole robot IPv4 and default route after old detach. This is one host, not two robots or a demonstrated wireless roam. The teammate's separate AP model and its exact semantics are not available in our checkout.

## Work completed

The consolidation matrix has 61 records: 57 successful recorded single-robot executions, two earlier multi-move workload executions and two failed setup/instrumentation attempts. These are heterogeneous evidence; do not pool them. Phase 0 includes the verified single move and a 13-test baseline. Phase 1A has two retained successful MBB smoke event sets. Phase 1B contains 20 matrix trials (five per BBM, MBB-100/250/500) and four pilots. Week 5 has nine exploratory IP-relearning trials, 20 controlled causal trials (five per treatment) and a separate access-port check. Older workload records have seven moves each, not seven independent topology trials. Fixed-order tests, sampling and source provenance constrain interpretation.

The Phase 0 move showed 116 controller probes, one timeout and a sampled Leaf5 robot /32 gap while a /24 remained. A source-specific lookup/RMAC/FDB sample resolved Leaf3, then later Leaf2. The observations are non-atomic and the timeout cause is unproven. Phase 1B loss vectors were BBM `0,0,0,1,1`, MBB100 `0,0,0,0,0`, MBB250 `0,0,0,1,0`, MBB500 `0,1,1,0,3`; these small samples do not establish a reliability ranking. A missing /32 and Leaf3 lookup were sampled in 4/5 MBB100 and 5/5 MBB500 matrix runs; nonsampling is not evidence of no transient. Phase 1 longer overlap sometimes sampled sequence 3, without a sampled duplicate flag.

The October workload's own CSV said no DAD warning, but Zebra logged duplicate MAC/IP warnings during a later return, outside that check. Forwarding/freeze impact was not established. DAD for the newer reported experiments was held enabled at 5 moves/180 seconds. Do not conflate that fixed baseline with a DAD sweep.

The fleet audit found no teammate report, named AP scripts or raw datasets in the searched checkout, local refs, nearby files and checked container directory. No AP fleet reproduction or threshold is established; this is not proof of absence elsewhere. Our earlier two-host workload serialized the link commands and is not their fleet-scale model.

In nine exploratory Week 5 BBM trials Leaf2 sampled the MAC locally while the IP neighbor and Leaf5 /32 were unavailable. Controller loss occurred in seven. Some repeated/delayed gratuitous ARP trials recovered packet delivery without a sampled restored /32, and one gateway-refresh trial showed ARP exchange followed by local IP, Type-2 and /32 recovery.

The causal study ran five fresh topologies each of no deliberate ARP, one immediate gateway request, one delayed request, and four repeated gateway requests, plus one separately delayed access-port instrumentation check. Probe-loss vectors were none `68,69,1,76,70`; immediate `67,70,1,75,70`; delayed `1,18,19,19,18`; repeated `0,1,0,1,1`. Later local-IP and Leaf5 /32-via-leaf2 samples appeared in 1/5, 1/5, 5/5, 5/5 respectively. In one delayed trial recovery preceded the scheduled request, so background traffic is a material confound. Five runs per condition and fixed order do not establish general probabilities.

## Current causal position

A pre-move/early Leaf2 sample showed the robot's remote externally learned `NOARP` neighbor. ARP was captured at the robot and Leaf2 bridge; the direct-port validation saw a robot ARP before a later neighbor deletion, with zero reported packet and netlink socket drops. Later local neighbor, MAC/IP advertisement and /32 restoration were observed in recovery runs. Upstream Linux v6.8 source contains a nonadministrative `NOARP` update guard, and FRR intentionally avoids blindly uninstalling remote neighbors on MAC change. These observations make the proposed kernel-neighbor/Zebra interaction plausible. They do not trace the running kernel update call/return, its exact neighbor state or Zebra's event decision, and exact vendor build/source linkage is unverified. Current decision: **CAUSAL MECHANISM PARTIALLY SUPPORTED**. No FRR defect, fix or production-capacity result is established.

A Leaf5 `/24` fallback can select Leaf3 when the robot is on Leaf2. One failed probe was seen reaching Leaf3, but its drop location is unproven. Another captured request traversed Leaf5→Leaf3 on VNI500, Leaf3→Leaf2 on VNI1000 and reached the robot, whose reply returned. Wrong first VTEP is not necessarily loss. UPDATE messages, peer NLRI transmissions, unique routes, withdrawals and TCP retransmissions must be counted separately. PCAPs use realtime while command/probe records use monotonic; some worker clock pairs exist but no full error bound. Samples are sequential and non-atomic. Controller probe loss is not an exact outage duration.
